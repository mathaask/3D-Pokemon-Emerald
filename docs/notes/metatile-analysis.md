# Emerald map data → 3D: metatile / elevation analysis

Status: 2026-10-16. Data source: the BPEE rev 0 ROM, decoded by `src/game/rom/*`. Every number below
comes from the 518 maps (`npx tsx tools/map-atlas.ts` → `out/atlas/stats.txt`) or from scratch
queries over the same decoders. Image paths are relative to the repo root and are regenerated by
the atlas tool (`out/` is gitignored).

This note is for whoever writes the overworld mesh builder (`src/render`). It covers what the map
data encodes, what it leaves out, and a concrete per-cell classification and height solver, with
known failure cases.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 0. Summary

1. **Elevation is a collision layer, not a height.** Walkable ground is elevation 3 almost everywhere,
   including tops of plateaus: Route 112's mountain plateau, Route 111's rock steps and cave
   floors are all 3. Blocked cells (trees, walls, cliffs, roofs) are almost always elevation 0.
   Elevations 4-9 only show up where the game must keep two walkable levels apart (bridges,
   Fortree decks, cycling road, a few terraced cities and dungeons). Where they do show up they
   usually increase with height (Mossdeep 5 < 7 < 9), but don't rely on it (Route 114 has separate
   plateaus at 4 and at 5).
2. **Heights have to come from the picture structure.** The usable signal is the run of blocked
   "cliff face" rows south of a plateau. A face that is *n* rows tall means the plateau is about
   *n* tiles higher than the ground in front of it. Bounded face runs (outdoor): 1 row 697,
   2 rows 280, 3 rows 169, 4 rows 111. Longer runs are mostly solid rock masses, not taller cliffs.
3. **The top layer is the 3D hint.** In NORMAL and SPLIT metatiles the top layer goes to BG1, which
   draws over the player. On passable cells, that is the part of a tall object (tree crown, roof
   ridge, bridge side) that projects north past the object's footprint: 75% of passable cells with
   top-layer pixels have a blocked cell directly south. COVERED metatiles draw both layers under
   the player, so they are flat ground decoration (flowers, shorelines, rims, ledges).
4. **Buildings** are a block of blocked rows with the door (`MB_ANIMATED_DOOR`) in the bottom row,
   plus one passable "roof overhang" row above. The most common outdoor building is 3 blocked
   rows + 1 overhang (54 doors: Pokémon Center, Mart, small houses), then 4 + 1 (19: Littleroot
   houses, Birch's lab, Rustboro houses). Wall/roof rows aren't marked in the data. Use the bottom
   2 rows as wall and the rest as roof.
5. **Trees**: narrow trees are 1 wide × 2 tall (passable crown overhang + blocked trunk). Round trees
   are 2 × 3 (overhang + 2 blocked rows). Dense forest uses fill tiles (0x0C6/0x0C7) where one tree's
   crown overlaps the next tree's trunk. Tree crowns on the overhang row are passable, so the player
   walks *behind* trees.
6. **Ledges** (`MB_JUMP_*`) are always collision 1 with elevation 0 (or 3), and the ground on both
   sides has the *same* elevation (of ~650 JUMP_SOUTH cells, 417 are 3/0/3 and 123 are 3/3/3). Treat them as small
   visual lips (about 0.5 tile), not as level changes.
7. **Water** is elevation 1 (surf layer). Shore cells are elevation 0 or 3 with water behaviours.
   Bridges are bridge behaviours (0x70-0x7F) at elevation 15 (multi-level) or 4, and the bridge's
   side face is painted into the top layer of the passable water cells next to it.
8. **Collision** in map data is only ever 0 or 1 (no 2/3 values in any of the 518 maps).

## 1. Data model recap

Map grid cell (u16): metatile id bits 0-9, collision bits 10-11, elevation bits 12-15
(`blockMetatile/blockCollision/blockElevation` in `src/game/rom/maps.ts`). Outside the layout,
the game shows the 2×2 border blocks with collision forced on (`blockAt()`).

Metatile = 8 tile entries: 4 bottom-layer, then 4 top-layer (TL, TR, BL, BR). Attributes:
behaviour (bits 0-7), layer type (bits 12-15). Ids 0-511 come from the primary tileset and
512-1023 from the secondary. The metatile count isn't stored; `loadTileset` takes it from the
gap between `metatiles` and `metatileAttributes`. That matched the symbol sizes for all 73
tilesets in use.

How layer types map to hardware layers (`field_camera.c: DrawMetatile`). BG priorities are
BG1 = 1, BG2 = 2, BG3 = 3, and objects are sprites with priority 2:

| layer type | bottom layer → | top layer → | meaning for 3D |
|---|---|---|---|
| NORMAL (0) | BG2 (under objects) | BG1 (**over** objects) | top layer = something above head height |
| COVERED (1) | BG3 | BG2 (under objects) | both layers are flat ground/decoration |
| SPLIT (2) | BG3 | BG1 (over objects) | rare (192 outdoor cells: water edges, 0x02D/0x098-0x09A ponds) |

For NORMAL metatiles the game writes tile 0x014/palette 3 into BG3, and it shows through
transparent bottom-layer pixels (General 0x0EE/0x0EF). `TilesetPair.drawMetatile(..., backdrop=true)`
reproduces this.

Object (sprite) priority comes from the object's elevation (`sElevationToPriority`):
`[2,2,2,2,1,2,1,2,1,2,1,2,1,0,0,2]`. Objects at even elevations 4/6/8/10/12 draw *over* BG1.
That is how a player on a bridge or treetop deck (elevation 4) draws over rails and crowns.
Movement rule (`IsElevationMismatchAt`): an object may enter a cell if the cell's elevation equals
its own, or either side is 0 (transition), or the cell is 15 (multi-level: the object keeps its
previous elevation, so you can walk on a bridge and surf under it).

Layer usage counts (cells; outdoor = town/city/route/ocean-route maps):

| | top layer empty, passable | empty, blocked | has top pixels, passable | has top pixels, blocked |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| outdoor NORMAL | 32 600 | 26 494 | **4 413** (overhangs) | 17 862 (roofs, forest, walls) |
| outdoor COVERED | 45 919 (mostly water) | 1 643 | 8 481 (flowers, shores) | 19 483 (rims, ledges, fences, sea rocks) |
| indoor NORMAL | 22 901 | 10 007 | 2 164 | 2 578 |
| indoor COVERED | 1 640 | 2 917 | 3 847 | 8 622 (furniture) |

Collision × elevation:
- Outdoor blocked cells: 89.8% have elevation 0 (58 828), 6.3% elevation 1 (rocks in the sea),
  3.8% elevation 3 (signs, boulders and lamp posts: single "prop" cells keep the ground elevation).
- Outdoor passable cells: only 3.3% have elevation 0. Those are transition cells: stair bottoms,

- Indoor: blocked 86% elevation 0; passable 77% elevation 3, 16% elevation 0 (exit mats

- Caves: blocked 96.5% elevation 0; 4 001 passable `MB_CAVE` cells at elevation 0 (transitions
  between cave levels).

### 2.2 What each value means visually

| elev | meaning | 3D interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | "transition / wall": blocked scenery, or passable cells joining two levels (stair bottoms, ladders, door mats, shores) | blocked: comes from the classifier; passable: slope or connector between neighbours |
| 1 | surf layer: all deep, ocean and pond water (43 576 OCEAN_WATER cells), waterfalls, currents | water surface, *below* ground |
| 2 | essentially unused (28 cave cells) | — |
| 3 | default ground, both lowland **and** most plateaus | floor; height from the solver (section 9) |
| 4 | upper walkway: bridges (Route 110 cycling road ends, Route 119/120), Fortree decks and rope bridges, Route 114 west plateau, PC escalator top, Acro-bike rails | raised deck, about 2 tiles above the ground it crosses |
| 5, 7, 9 | terraces where height really increases (Mossdeep 5/7/9, Ever Grande 5/7, Lilycove 5, Route 114/115 5/7, Route 120 5) | higher plateaus, ordered by value within one map |
| 6 | 54 cells on the Battle Pyramid top, 1 outdoor cell | — |
| 15 | multi-level: bridge decks over walkable or surfable cells (Route 110 185 cells, Route 120 47, Route 119 22, Fortree 4), Shoal Cave and Victory Road crossings | deck at the level of the neighbouring 4/upper cells, with the lower level passing underneath |

Elevation changes between adjacent walkable cells (excluding 0/15) are almost all 3↔1
(ground↔water, about 3 200 pairs). Then 3↔5 (138), 3↔4 (155), 1↔4 (61), 4↔5 (28), 4↔6 (28,
indoor). Different levels are normally separated by blocked cells or 0/15 connectors, not by
direct steps.

Examples:
- `out/atlas/debug/0_9_LittlerootTown_elevation.png`: houses and trees 0, ground 3, top roof
  row 3 (passable), truck spot 15.
- `out/atlas/debug/0_27_Route112_elevation.png`: the whole mountain plateau and the lowland are both 3.
- `out/atlas/debug/0_6_MossdeepCity_elevation.png`: terraces 5 (west), 7 (east), 9 (space centre),
  stairs with 0 at the bottom row.
- `out/atlas/debug/0_25_Route110_elevation.png`: cycling road is 15 over water and ground, 4 over
  the fence rows. Water underneath is 1.
- `out/atlas/debug/0_4_FortreeCity_elevation.png`: decks and rope bridges 4, ladders 0, ground 3,
  rope bridge over a path 15.
- `out/atlas/debug/0_29_Route114_elevation.png`: separate plateaus at 4 and 5.

## 3. Cliffs, rock walls, ledges

### 3.1 How a raised area is drawn (General "mountain" autotile, grass variant)

Reading a column from north to south through a walkable plateau:

```
lowland (3)                      passable
north RIM row                    blocked, COVERED, top layer = rock outline over grass   (0x068 0x069 0x06A, 0x080-0x082)
plateau surface                  0x071 MOUNTAIN_TOP (passable if walkable, else solid rock top)
...
south FACE rows (n rows)         blocked, NORMAL, bottom layer only                       (0x074 0x089 0x07C 0x079 0x091 ...)
face bottom corners              blocked, COVERED, top layer = rock, bottom = ground      (0x078 0x07A 0x090 0x092 0x0AB 0x0AC)
lowland (3)                      passable
```

West and east sides are 1-column strips (0x070/0x088 on the west, 0x072/0x08A on the east).
Faces only exist on the **south** side. A plateau's north edge is just the rim row, with no
visible drop, because that face would point away from the camera.

Measured from bounded vertical blocked runs in outdoor maps (scratch prototype, all outdoor maps):
- Face runs whose top cell is not a rim (walkable plateau directly above the face):
  1 row 697, 2 rows 280, 3 rows 169, 4 rows 111, 5 rows 71, 6+ rows ~170.
  - 1-row walls: Route 111 / Rustboro rock steps, using `0x079` (RockWall_GrassBase, 1 090 uses,
    83% passable to the south) and `0x07C` (RockWall_RockBase).
    See `out/atlas/0_26_Route111.png` rows 120-126 (x 13-27): two 1-row steps with 1-row stairs 0x0AF/0x0CF.
  - 2-row walls: caves (Granite Cave, `out/atlas/24_7_GraniteCave_1F.png`) and many General mountains.
  - 3-row walls: Route 112 tiers (Lavaridge tileset 0x274/0x279), with 3-row stairs at x 20-21, y 41-43
    (`out/atlas/0_27_Route112.png`).
  - Runs ≥5 are mostly *solid* mountains (rim at the top, face at the bottom), where rim detection
    failed (see failure cases).
- Runs that start with a rim (solid rock masses with ground on both sides): 2-8 rows typical.

**Can the height difference be seen in the data?** Only through the face-row count (and the stairs
length, which equals it). Elevation is identical (3) above and below in 95%+ of cases. Exceptions
are the terraced maps listed in 2.2, where the higher terrace has a higher elevation value.

Secondary tilesets have their own rock autotiles with the same structure: Lavaridge 0x26x-0x27x
(Route 112, Jagged Pass, Mt Chimney), Fallarbor 0x20x-0x26x (Route 113/114 ash), Mauville/Fortree
sets on Routes 111/119/120, Sootopolis crater 0x2E1, Mossdeep 0x33x (sea cliffs).
`out/atlas/tilesets/<Tileset>_<first id>.png` shows each set (left: both layers, right: top layer
only on magenta; label = id, layer type letter, behaviour hex).

### 3.2 Ledges

- Behaviours `MB_JUMP_SOUTH/EAST/WEST/SOUTHEAST/SOUTHWEST` (0x38-0x3F). Collision is **always 1**
  (the jump overrides it). The layer is COVERED, and the ledge lip is in the top layer.
- Elevation context (N / ledge / S): JUMP_SOUTH 3/0/3 417×, 3/3/3 123×. Side ledges (JUMP_EAST/WEST)
  form vertical lines (0/0/0 along the line).
- The ledge row is always 1 row. Route 101: `out/atlas/debug/0_16_Route101_behavior.png` (orange, 3B).
- 3D: a lip or wedge about 0.5 tile high on the ledge cell, dropping toward the jump direction. Only
  raise the upper side by 0.5 when the two sides are separate walkable components. Usually they're
  the same component (you can walk around the ledge), so the ledge should not change floor heights.

### 3.3 Stairs

Stairs have behaviour NORMAL and collision 0, so the behaviour doesn't identify them. They show up
as passable columns cutting through a face run, flanked west and east by blocked face cells. General
0x0AF (left/edge) and 0x0CF (centre), 1-3 rows long. Elevation is the same as the levels they join
(3), or 0 on the bottom row when the levels differ (Mossdeep 9→7: `0AF/0CF` at elevation 9, then
the bottom row at 0). Stair length equals the face height next to it.

## 4. Trees

Two General tree families. All ids are General (primary); secondaries have their own (Fortree
palms, Route 119 jungle).

| part | ids | collision | layer / top | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| narrow tree crown on the overhang row | 0x00E, 0x00F (over grass), 0x01D, 0x025 (over tall grass, `TallGrass_TreeUp`), 0x01E, 0x01F, 0x0F6, 0x1E8, 0x1E9 | **passable** (0-5%), elev 3 | NORMAL, top ~200/256 px | the player walks behind the tree; crown drawn over the player |
| narrow tree trunk / lower crown | 0x016, 0x017 | blocked, elev 0 | NORMAL, bottom only | 66% have passable ground to the south |
| forest fill (crown of tree above + trunk of this tree) | 0x0C6 (3 942 uses), 0x0C7 (3 051), 0x0CE (crown over blocked cell) | blocked, elev 0 | NORMAL, both layers fully opaque | runs of these form dense forest; the run's bottom cell is 0x016/0x017 |
| round tree overhang row | 0x1CE, 0x1CF (`Grass_TreeLeft/Right`), 0x1C6, 0x1C7 (over tall grass) | passable | NORMAL, top layer | |
| round tree top half | 0x1D4, 0x1D5 (another tree above), 0x1D6, 0x1D7 (top-most, grass behind) | blocked, elev 0 | NORMAL, no top layer | |
| round tree bottom half | 0x1DC, 0x1DD (another tree below), 0x1E4-0x1E7 (lowest, grass base), 0x1EC, 0x1ED | blocked | NORMAL, tiny top layer (23 px tufts) | stacks: `[1D4,1DC,1D4,1E4]` = two trees |
| variants at water, sand or rock edges | 0x1F0-0x1F5, 0x1FC, 0x1FD, 0x040, 0x083/0x084 | blocked | | worth a quick visual check |

Sizes: a narrow tree is 1 wide × 2 tall (1 passable + 1 blocked). A round tree is 2 wide × 3 tall
(1 passable + 2 blocked). The narrow tree's "trunk" cell holds the lower half of the crown and the
trunk. The upper half is the overhang above it.

Layer types: trees are NORMAL throughout. Blocked tree cells keep elevation 0. Crown overhang cells
keep the ground elevation (3). Tree tops are passable only on the overhang row. Every blocked tree
cell can't be walked on.

Examples: `out/atlas/0_9_LittlerootTown.png` (narrow trees on the edges),
`out/atlas/0_10_OldaleTown.png` (round trees), `out/atlas/0_17_Route102.png`,
`out/atlas/24_11_PetalburgWoods.png` (forest interior),
`out/atlas/debug/0_9_LittlerootTown_layer.png` (crown rows marked "NT").

## 5. Buildings

Rules seen in every town:
- The door is in the **bottom row** of the building. `MB_ANIMATED_DOOR` (General 0x021 house,
  0x041 Mart, 0x061 Pokémon Center, 0x1CD Gym, 0x1DB Contest; secondary e.g. Petalburg 0x248/0x249/0x287)
  is collision 1, elevation 0, and the warp is on it. `MB_NON_ANIMATED_DOOR` is used for passable
  openings: cave entrances (General 0x0A7 under 0x09F), Fortree tree-house doors, Rustboro
  two-wide doors, Route 104 flower shop. Those cells are collision 0, so look one cell north for
  the building.
- Blocked rows above the door (door row included), then passable overhang rows with a top layer:

  | blocked / overhang | count | examples |
  |---|---|---|
  | 3 / 1 | 54 | Pokémon Center, Mart, Oldale/Petalburg houses (4 columns wide) |
  | 4 / 1 | 19 | Littleroot houses (5 wide), Birch's lab (7 wide), Rustboro houses (6-7 wide) |
  | 4 / 0, 5 / 0 | 16 | Lilycove (building against another block) |
  | 6-9 / 0-1 | ~25 | Gyms (Petalburg Gym: 9 rows, door 0x1CD) and large city buildings (Rustboro, Mauville, Lilycove, Fortree) |
  | 13-15 | 3 | the largest Slateport/Lilycove buildings |

  NON_ANIMATED_DOOR: 57 × "0 blocked / 0 overhang" (the opening sits in a cliff or tree face).
- **Sizes (General built-ins):**
  - Pokémon Center: 4 × 4 cells. Overhang row 0x048-0x04B (secondaries swap the edge cells, e.g.
    Petalburg 0x282/0x283), roof 0x050-0x053, roof lower/sign 0x058-0x05B, wall+door 0x060-0x063 (door 0x061).
  - Mart: 4 × 4. Overhang 0x028-0x02B, 0x030-0x033, 0x038-0x03B, wall+door 0x040-0x043 (door 0x041;
    left cell often 0x060).
  - Gym: General 0x1AA-0x1BC (flat tan roof), facade 0x1C0-0x1CD (door 0x1CD), 5-9 rows tall.
  - Houses come from the secondary tileset: Petalburg 0x26C-0x28F (4 × 4: overhang 0x26C-0x26E,
    roof 0x274-0x276, roof+window 0x27C-0x27F, wall+door 0x284-0x287). Littleroot houses 0x208-0x24A (5 × 5).
- **Roof vs wall** isn't encoded in collision, elevation or behaviour (all roof and wall cells are
  collision 1, elevation 0, NORMAL). Weak cues: the overhang row and the top roof row have top-layer
  pixels across the whole width. Wall rows have top-layer pixels only in the edge columns (the
  side walls) and none in the middle. See the Littleroot dump: row 5 `210-212 NT` all T, row 6
  `219 N.` in the middle, rows 7-8 the wall. Practical rule: **wall = the bottom 2 rows (door row
  and the row above), roof = everything above, plus the passable overhang row**. Gyms and large
  buildings have taller facades (3-4 rows), so override per secondary tileset if needed.
- Indoor/outdoor size relation: none in the data. Interiors are separate maps.

Examples: `out/atlas/0_10_OldaleTown.png` (PC at x 5-8, y 13-16; Mart x 13-16, y 3-6; houses),
`out/atlas/0_9_LittlerootTown.png` + `_layer`/`_collision` debug,
`out/atlas/0_0_PetalburgCity.png`, `out/atlas/0_3_RustboroCity.png`.

## 6. Other features

- **Water.** Surf layer elevation 1: OCEAN_WATER 43 576 cells, POND_WATER 1 817, DEEP_WATER
  (dive) 3 352, currents 4 383, WATERFALL 277. Edges and shores are COVERED metatiles. The top layer
  holds the shoreline (e.g. 0x0B0-0x0C4 pond edges, 0x16x/0x17x sea edges). Shore cells are
  elevation 0 (2 293 OCEAN_WATER at 0) or 3 (SHALLOW_WATER 3 216 at 3, walkable).
  `NO_SURFACING`/`SEAWEED` underwater use elevation 3. Rocks in the sea: General 0x150/0x151/0x158/0x159
  (2 × 2 boulder, the most common blocked run in Slateport/Route 105-109), 0x175/0x176 + 0x17D/0x17E
  (rock edge), blocked at elevation 1 or 0. Waterfalls (0x04C, 0x0B3, 0x0B9, 0x0BB, 0x0BC) are vertical
  water faces: render them as a vertical animated plane between the two water levels.
- **Tall grass** `MB_TALL_GRASS` (0x00D; 0x025/0x1C6/0x1C7 also carry tree overhangs) and
  `MB_LONG_GRASS` (0x015, Route 119/120). NORMAL, no top layer: the grass over the player's legs
  is a field-effect sprite, not tile data. Render floor plus optional grass blades.
  `MB_ASHGRASS` on Route 113 (secondary).
- **Sand.** General 0x118-0x12A (beach paths; behaviour mostly NORMAL), `MB_SAND` 0x11C/0x124/0x13D/0x13E.
  Desert `MB_DEEP_SAND` and footprints come from secondaries (Route 111 Mauville set).
- **Fences.** 1-row blocked horizontal runs with passable cells north and south. General 0x148-0x14A
  (white posts, COVERED), 0x140-0x142, 0x03E/0x04E (Route 110 and Rustboro fence lines),
  secondary fences (Rustboro 0x2Fx, Fortree railings). Thin upright alpha-tested quads about
  0.6 tile high.
- **Signs, lamps, boulders.** Single blocked cells with passable N/S/E/W neighbours, elevation 3
  (they keep the ground elevation). General 0x003 is the standard route sign (114 uses).
  Small rocks are 0x0E0/0x0E2. A `bg_event` of kind 0-4 on a blocked cell is a sign or
  interactable. Upright billboard, 1 tile high, plus the overhang above if any.
- **Flowers.** 0x004 (COVERED, top layer = flowers, animated by the tileset callback). Passable.
  Flat decoration, or tiny billboards.
- **Bridges.** Deck cells have bridge behaviours: `BRIDGE_OVER_OCEAN` 0x70 (Route 110 cycling road,
  Route 119), `BRIDGE_OVER_POND_LOW/MED/HIGH` 0x71-0x73 (Route 120), `FORTREE_BRIDGE` 0x78,
  `BIKE_BRIDGE_OVER_BARRIER` 0x7F, Pacifidlog logs 0x74-0x77. Elevation 15 over surfable or walkable
  cells, 4 where it crosses fences or ends on ground. The bridge's **side face, pillars and rails
  are painted into the top layer of the passable cells next to it**: Route 110 rows 32-33 are
  surfable water (elevation 1) with NORMAL top layer showing the deck's south face and pillars
  (`out/atlas/0_25_Route110.png`). In 3D, lift those top-layer pixels onto a vertical plane under the
  deck's south edge and show plain water under them. Deck height: about 1.5-2 tiles over water,
  higher for Route 120's high bridge (`out/atlas/0_35_Route120.png`, `out/atlas/0_34_Route119.png`).
- **Ladders.** Fortree: passable elevation-0 cells with `MB_NO_RUNNING` (0x0A) joining ground (3)
  and deck (4). The deck sits about 2 rows above (ladder length). Indoor ladders `MB_LADDER` (0x61).
- **Slopes.** `MB_MUDDY_SLOPE` 0x0E8-0x0EB (Route 113/Jagged), `MB_BUMPY_SLOPE` 0x1F8/0x1F9: ramps.
  Acro-bike rails 0x0EC-0x0FD (elevation 4).
- **Ice, cracked floor, puddles.** Floors with a material flag (`ICE`, `THIN_ICE`, `CRACKED_FLOOR`,
  `PUDDLE` 0x0C8-0x0DA at elevation 3: reflective).

## 7. Indoor maps

- Primary tileset `Building` has only 8 metatiles. Everything else comes from the secondary
  (`PokemonCenter`, `Shop`, `BrendansMaysHouse`, `Lab`, `GenericBuilding`, ...).
- **Back wall** = blocked rows at the top. Counted per column (first non-void cell down to the
  first walkable cell): 2 rows in 2 101 columns, 3 in 497, 4 in 347, 5 in 194, 1 in 165. So the
  wall is **2 rows**: row 0 is the wall top/cornice, row 1 the wall face with windows, posters,
  PC, region map. Furniture against the wall adds rows (3-5). Examples:
  `out/atlas/2_2_OldaleTown_PokemonCenter_1F.png` (14×9: wall rows 0-1, counter rows 2-3 with
  `MB_COUNTER` at the nurse, escalator `MB_UP_ESCALATOR` at (1,6) elevation 4, exit mat
  `MB_SOUTH_ARROW_WARP` at (6-7,8)), `out/atlas/1_0_LittlerootTown_BrendansHouse_1F.png`
  (kitchen furniture rows 1-2, stairs `NON_ANIMATED_DOOR` at (8,2)),
  `out/atlas/1_4_LittlerootTown_ProfessorBirchsLab.png`.
- Side walls are 1-column blocked strips (often NORMAL with top layer, so the player draws behind
  the wall edge). Void cells (both layers empty or black) outside the room: render nothing, or a
  black ceiling.
- **Furniture**: blocked cells inside the room, mostly COVERED with top layer (8 622 cells).
  Behaviours identify interactables: `COUNTER` 0x80 (474 blocked + 92 passable), `PC` 0x83, `TELEVISION`
  0x86, `REGION_MAP` 0x85, `BOOKSHELF`/`SHOP_SHELF`/`PICTURE_BOOK_SHELF` 0xE0-0xE5, `VASE` 0xE3,
  `TRASH_CAN` 0xE4. Tables and beds are plain NORMAL blocked cells. Passable furniture tops
  (NORMAL + top layer, 2 164 cells) are the upper parts of tall furniture (plants, bookshelves)
  drawn over the player, the same overhang rule as outdoors.
- Indoor elevations: floor 3, walls and furniture 0, a few blocked furniture cells at 1/3, escalator
  and stair tops 4, elevation-0 passable cells for exits and stairs.

## 8. Caves

Floor 3, walls 0 (96.5% of blocked cells). Cave walls are 2-row faces on the south side of each
rock mass (`out/atlas/24_7_GraniteCave_1F.png`). Multi-level caves use 4/5 and 15 crossings
(Meteor Falls, Victory Road, Shoal Cave, Artisan Cave). Rock masses are flat-topped (render them
as extruded blocks about 2 tiles tall). For a ceiling-less look, cap the blocks with darkened
rock.

## 9. Recommended reconstruction

### 9.1 Per-cell classes

```
FLOOR(h, material)         passable ground at height h (material: grass, tallgrass, sand, ash, ice, path, ...)
OVERHANG                   FLOOR, but the top layer belongs to the object south of it (crown, roof ridge, bridge side)
WATER(level)               surf-layer cell; level = shore floor height - 0.25
STAIRS(h0, h1)             passable ramp/steps between two heights
LEDGE(dir)                 thin lip, 0.5 high, collision 1
BRIDGE(h)                  deck slab at h, with the lower level passing underneath
WALL_FACE(base, height)    south-facing terrain face (cliff, cave wall, sea cliff)
ROCK_BLOCK(base, height)   unwalkable raised terrain (rim + body + face): extruded block with top cap
BUILDING(id)               part of a building block (footprint, wall rows, roof rows)
TREE(kind)                 narrow (1×2) or round (2×3) tree, forest fill
PROP_BILLBOARD             sign, lamp, boulder, mailbox: upright quad
FENCE                      thin upright quad
INDOOR_WALL(height)        back and side walls of interiors
FURNITURE(height)          blocked interior cell: box or billboard from its rows
VOID                       empty metatile / outside the room
```

### 9.2 Inputs per cell

`id, col, elev, beh, layerType, topCov, botCov` (`TilesetPair.layerCoverage(id, top)`),
`bgEvent` at the cell, map type, neighbours. Plus a **role table** per (tileset, metatile)
computed offline over all maps and overridable by hand:
`role ∈ {RIM, FACE, FACE_BASE, SIDE_W, SIDE_E, PLATEAU, TREE_*, ROOF_*, FENCE, STAIRS, PROP, ...}`.
Usage signatures make a good auto-classifier: the fraction of uses with passable north/south/west/east
neighbours, average position inside vertical blocked runs, layer type and top coverage, plus a
green-pixel ratio for trees. Section 10 lists these signatures for the General ids. Ship the table
as generated data (ids only, no pixels) in `src/game/` once tuned.

### 9.3 Rules (order matters)

```
for each cell:
  if beh in 0x70..0x7F or (elev == 15 and outdoor)          -> BRIDGE
  elif beh in JUMP_* (0x38..0x3F)                            -> LEDGE(dir)
  elif beh in WATER_SET or (elev == 1 and col == 0)          -> WATER
       WATER_SET = POND/INTERIOR_DEEP/DEEP/WATERFALL/SOOTOPOLIS_DEEP/OCEAN/NO_SURFACING/currents/SEAWEED*
  elif col == 0:
       STAIRS if role==STAIRS or (W and E neighbours are blocked terrain and the column crosses a face run)
       else FLOOR (+OVERHANG flag if layerType != COVERED and topCov > 0)
  else (col == 1):
       BUILDING if in a building component (below)
       TREE     if role in TREE_*      (fallback: >45% green pixels)
       FENCE    if role==FENCE         (fallback: vertical run length 1, horizontal run >= 3, N and S passable)
       PROP     if bgEvent sign here, or vertical and horizontal run length 1 with N and S passable
       INDOOR_WALL / FURNITURE for indoor maps (top blocked rows = wall)
       else TERRAIN (role RIM / FACE / FACE_BASE / SIDE / PLATEAU-solid)
```

**Building components**: start at each door cell (ANIMATED_DOOR, PETALBURG_GYM_DOOR; for passable
NON_ANIMATED_DOOR start at the cell north of it). Flood-fill 4-connected blocked cells upward and
sideways, never below the door row. Stop at cells whose role is TREE/FENCE/TERRAIN or that come
from the primary tileset with a rock role. Then add the passable overhang row directly above the
top blocked row in the component's columns. The footprint is the blocked cells (depth = blocked
rows, e.g. 3 for a PC).

### 9.4 Height solver (validated on Route 101/111/112/114/120, Mossdeep, Rustboro, Granite Cave)

```
1. Walkable cells = FLOOR/OVERHANG/STAIRS/BRIDGE (not WATER, not blocked).
   Components: 4-connected, same elevation value only (0 and 15 cells form their own components,
   STAIRS components are kept separate).
2. Constraints, from every column: walkable A (north) | blocked run R | walkable B (south):
     R all LEDGE                              -> h(A) = h(B) + 0.5   (weak; skip if A == B)
     R all TERRAIN and R[0] is not a RIM      -> h(A) = h(B) + len(R)          (cliff face)
     R all TERRAIN and R[0] is a RIM          -> h(A) = h(B)  (solid block between two grounds), block height = #FACE rows at the bottom of R
     R all TREE/BUILDING/PROP                 -> h(A) = h(B)
     anything else                            -> no constraint
   STAIRS component of length n between A and B -> h(A) = h(B) + n.
   Elevation hint (terraced maps): if elev(A) > elev(B) > 2 and both are in {3,5,7,9}, require h(A) > h(B).
3. Aggregate per component pair: take the MODE of the proposed d (not first-come), weight = support × min(size).
   Weighted union-find with offsets, heaviest pairs first. Count conflicts and ignore them.
4. Anchor: per connected set, min height = 0. Across maps: the component touching the
   connection edge must match the neighbour map (usually 0 = "sea-level ground").
5. Water level = min(height of adjacent walkable components) - 0.25. Bridges: deck = h(ends) + 1.5,
   or follow the elevation-4 neighbours.
6. Unconstrained components: take the height of the dominant neighbouring component.
```

Prototype results (scratch, `out/tmp/proto.ts`, not shipped): Route 112's plateau comes out +2
over the lowland (the stairs say 3, because the mode over corner columns picked 2, so prefer
STAIRS/central columns). Mossdeep's 5/7 terraces separate correctly. Rustboro is flat except its
waterside. Granite Cave gets two levels.

### 9.5 Geometry

Units: 1 metatile = 1 world unit; 1 face row = 1 unit of height (16 px = 16 px). The player
billboard is 2 units (32 px sprite).

- **Floors**: quad per cell at h, textured with the bottom layer. COVERED cells add their top layer.
  **OVERHANG cells use the bottom layer only**. Their top-layer pixels go to the object south of them.
- **Cliff faces**: put the vertical face (or a steep ~75° batter) at the **south edge of the face
  run**, height = run length, textured with the run's rows (both layers). Fill the cells of the
  face run at the top height with a cap that repeats the plateau texture (copy the cell above the
  run). This keeps lowland characters touching the cliff base, as in the GBA view. Plateau
  characters then stand `n` tiles back from the edge, which looks natural. Alternative: a 45° slope
  across the run. Contact is exact at both ends, but it reads as a slope, not a cliff.
- **Solid rock blocks**: extrude the run footprint to the face height. The top cap is textured with
  the rim and body rows, the front with the face rows. West/east side strips become side walls.
- **Buildings**: box over the footprint, front wall height 2 (the bottom 2 rows' pixels on the south
  face). The roof is a slope from (south edge, 2) to (north edge, 2 + 0.75 × roofRows), textured
  with the roof rows plus the overhang row. A perspective camera then projects the ridge past the
  footprint the way the overhang row does in the GBA view. The door is a recessed quad on the
  bottom row. Side walls: repeat the edge column texture.
- **Trees**: one instance per trunk cell (0x016/0x017 or each forest-fill cell; each 0x0C6 cell is
  one trunk), height 2, using the trunk cell plus the cell above as a crossed/billboard sprite.
  Round trees: one instance per 2×2 blocked block, height 3, from the 2×3 pixel block. Optional
  procedural 3D trees with the colours sampled from these pixels.
- **Props/fences**: upright quads, alpha from palette index 0. Fences sit on the cell's centre line.
- **Bridges**: deck slab at deck height. Side planes use the top-layer pixels of the adjacent
  passable cells (the ones next to the deck that carry rails or faces).
- **Water**: animated plane at the water level. Tile animations come from live VRAM (spec 3.3).
- **Indoor**: back wall = vertical quad at the south edge of the wall rows, height = wall rows
  (usually 2), texture = those rows. Side walls likewise. Furniture = boxes 1 unit tall, or
  billboards when they have a top layer.

### 9.6 Known failure cases

1. **Rim detection by signature misses** on some secondary rock sets. A solid mountain then reads as
   an enormous face (runs of 5 or more rows: ~260). Mitigation: cap face height at 4. Treat a longer run as
   ROCK_BLOCK with height = trailing FACE-role rows. Fix the role table for that tileset.
2. **Building flood-fill leaks** into fences and cliff strips that touch the building
   (`out/tmp/proto_RustboroCity_class.png`: the fence lines in Rustboro became "building").
   Stop at FENCE/rock roles and at 1-row horizontal runs.
3. **NON_ANIMATED_DOOR** cells are passable openings in cliffs and trees (Fortree houses, Rustboro
   2-wide doors, caves). Don't start buildings from them unless the cell north is a building role.
4. **Trees on cliffs or water** (0x0CE crown on a blocked cell, 0x1F0-0x1F5) mix TREE and TERRAIN in one
   vertical run. That breaks the "all TERRAIN" rule and drops the constraint (harmless but
   loses height). Allow a TREE prefix on a face run.
5. **Bridges over walkable ground** (Fortree rope bridge row 14, x 30-33 = 15; Route 110 deck over
   fences = 4): the cell is both "deck" and "ground under it". Render both.
6. **Elevation-0 passable cells** glue terraces if components are allowed to join across 0. Join
   only equal elevations.
7. **Water connectivity**: water must not join ground components (beaches at elevation 0 and 3 touch
   water directly), or everything collapses to one height.
8. **Ledges inside one component** produce contradictory +0.5 constraints. Use them only as visual lips.
9. **Map seams**: plateau heights computed per map can disagree at connections (Route 111/112/113 around
   Mt Chimney). Solve over the stitched neighbourhood, or anchor connection edges to 0.
10. **Special maps** need overrides: Sootopolis (crater ring, 0x2E1 faces), Pacifidlog (log bridges
    at water level), Sky Pillar, Mirage Tower, Battle Frontier, Trick House, underwater maps
    (elevation 0/3 only), Secret Bases.

## 11. Atlas / debug image legend

`npx tsx tools/map-atlas.ts [--only <name>] [--all-debug] [--no-sheets]`. About 2 minutes for
everything; most of that is PNG compression. Note: `npx` adds ~10 s on this machine, so
`node --import tsx tools/map-atlas.ts` is faster.

- `out/atlas/<g>_<n>_<Name>.png`: layout rendered like the game. Backdrop is black (palette 0
  colour 0 is forced to black by `LoadTilesetPalette`), NORMAL BG3 filler tile, then bottom layer,
  then top layer.
- `out/atlas/debug/*_collision.png`: red tint = collision 1.
- `*_elevation.png`: colour + hex digit per elevation; red frame = blocked.
- `*_layer.png`: label N/C/S, plus T if the top layer has opaque pixels; white bar = top-layer
  coverage. Blue tint = NORMAL with top pixels, green = COVERED, magenta = SPLIT.
- `*_behavior.png`: hex behaviour. Green = grass, blue = water/currents, red = directional
  impassable, orange = ledges, yellow = forced movement, magenta = doors/warps, brown = bridges,
  sand colour = sand, cyan = furniture/interactables, grey = cave/mountain-top.
- `out/atlas/tilesets/<Tileset>_<first id>.png`: 128 metatiles per sheet, 8 per row (the decomp's
  `METATILE_ROW_WIDTH`). Each cell is [both layers | top layer on magenta checkerboard], labelled
  `id  layer  behaviour`.
- `out/atlas/stats.txt` / `stats.json`: per-map-type elevation histograms, collision by behaviour,
  per-map elevation usage, and per-metatile usage (uses, maps, collision %, elevation histogram)
  for General and Building.
